Fixing Slow Downloads Caused by SQM on a GL.iNet Router with Hardware Acceleration

2026-11-03#networking#router#gl-inet#openwrt#sqm#troubleshooting

TL;DR: Large downloads on my home network were crawling at 20–200 KB/s. The router was not overloaded and the line was fine. The cause was SQM (Smart Queue Management) download shaping fighting with the Qualcomm hardware offload (ECM/NSS) on my GL.iNet router. Disabling SQM brought downloads back to 12–67 MB/s.

Symptoms

  • Big downloads on every device were stuck in the tens to low hundreds of KB/s.
  • It happened even when the router was idle, so it was not congestion from other traffic.
  • Small requests (web pages, APIs) felt mostly normal, which made the problem easy to miss: only sustained, high-throughput TCP transfers were hurt.

Measurements

Tested from a mini PC on the LAN, before and after disabling SQM:

TestWith SQMWithout SQM
Hetzner Singapore (wired)158 KB/s41.5 MB/s
OVH France (wired)22 KB/s12 MB/s
Wi-Fi (same host)–67 MB/s

That is a 100× to 500× improvement just from turning one feature off.

Root cause

The router uses Qualcomm hardware acceleration (ECM / NSS offload). With offload active, most packets of an established flow bypass the Linux kernel’s normal forwarding path and are handled by the accelerator.

Generic OpenWrt SQM (sqm-scripts, with cake or fq_codel) works by shaping traffic in the kernel, typically via an IFB device on the ingress side. Here it was configured to shape download at about 520 Mbit/s.

When both run at once, they work against each other:

  1. Part of the traffic is accelerated and never seen by the shaper, while other packets are queued and shaped in software.
  2. The shaper’s view of the flow becomes inconsistent, so it drops or delays packets in bursts.
  3. TCP sees the packet loss, triggers retransmissions, and collapses its congestion window.
  4. Long transfers never ramp back up, and throughput settles in the KB/s range.

So the “QoS” was not limiting bandwidth to 520 Mbit/s. It was breaking TCP entirely.

The fix

Disable SQM permanently on the WAN interface and restart the service:

uci set sqm.eth1.enabled=0
uci commit sqm
/etc/init.d/sqm restart

Verify that no shaping qdisc is left on the WAN or IFB interfaces:

tc qdisc show

Downloads recovered immediately and stayed fast after a reboot.

A second, unrelated issue: DNS rate limiting

While debugging I also found that the local DNS resolver had a rate limit enabled. Under bursty load (many parallel lookups from package managers, browsers, and update tools) it caused occasional resolution stalls that looked like “slow internet”. I turned the rate limit off as well. It was not the cause of the slow downloads, but it was another source of flaky behaviour worth removing.

Recommendations

  • On GL.iNet routers with hardware acceleration, do not use generic SQM. If you want QoS, use the QoS feature in the GL.iNet web UI, which is designed to work with the vendor’s acceleration path.
  • Only use sqm-scripts (cake / fq_codel) if you disable hardware acceleration first, and accept that software shaping will cost CPU and lower the maximum throughput.
  • When downloads are mysteriously slow on an otherwise idle network, test with sustained single-flow downloads from a few different regions. Short speed tests and page loads can hide the problem.
  • Check for retransmissions with ss -ti or netstat -s on the client: a high retransmit count with no real congestion points at a misbehaving shaper or offload path.
